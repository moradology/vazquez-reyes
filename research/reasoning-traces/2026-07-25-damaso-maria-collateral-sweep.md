# Dámaso Martínez and María Rivera collateral sweep

Research date: 25 July 2026

## Question

Can later Humacao civil registrations supply life details or additional
children for the Dámaso Martínez and María Rivera pair named as Ana
Martínez's parents in Mauricio Reyes Martínez's 1882 baptism?

## Method

The dedicated Ancestry cockpit was used to search Puerto Rico civil
registrations in both name orders. Every potentially relevant original was
downloaded under a unique capture name before interpretation.

## Defensible result

Humacao act 464 records the death of **Severiano Martínez Rivera** on
5 March 1922. He was reported as 60 and a native of Humacao. The original
names his parents as **Dámaso Martínez and María Rivera**, both natives of
Humacao, married, of full age, and already deceased.

That combination is materially stronger than a surname-only match:

- the parent pair exactly repeats the clearest 1882 grandparent pair;
- the place is Humacao, where Ana and her children are documented;
- Severiano's estimated 1862 birth falls in a plausible sibling generation
  for Ana, whose later records place her birth about 1854–1858.

It is still not a proof that the two records describe one unique parent
couple. Ana's own baptism or her marriage to Pedro Reyes remains the needed
identity bridge. Severiano is therefore recorded as a **probable collateral
child and probable brother**, not a confirmed sibling.

## Preserved but not merged

A separate 1886 Humacao death entry for Pedro Martínez Rivera continues onto
the next filmed page. That continuation appears to name his father as
**Damacio Martínez y Rivera** and his mother as María Rivera. The extra Rivera
surname attached to the father and the Damacio/Dámaso reading are not silently
normalized. Both filmed pages were retained, but Pedro was not attached to
Ana's family.

## Ramón Reyes and Inés Castro

The same pass tested Ramón Reyes with Inés Castro in both name orders. The
first-page civil-index results produced no record that combined the target
pair with Pedro, Humacao, or another reliable relationship anchor. This is a
limited first-page screen, not evidence of absence.

## Next record

Search Humacao baptisms around 1854–1858 and the Pedro Reyes–Ana Martínez
marriage around 1874–1875. A record that names Ana's parents directly would
either confirm Severiano as a sibling or keep the same-named parent couple
separate.
