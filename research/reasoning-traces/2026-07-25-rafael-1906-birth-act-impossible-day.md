# Rafael Vázquez: the impossible day in Humacao act 134

Date reviewed: 25 July 2026

## Question

Can Rafael Vázquez y Perales's full 1906 Humacao civil birth image resolve the
conflict among February 1906, 28 February, and the remembered 24 October?

## Original

The preserved full-resolution image is Humacao act 134. The act was registered
on 7 April 1906. Rafael's father, Juan Vázquez, appeared as the declarant.

The birth statement is legible across two lines:

> nació el día veinte y nueve de Febrero último

It says that the boy was born at 6 a.m. on 29 February 1906. The act later
states that the child was given the name Rafael and names his mother as Carlina
Perales. It also repeats the paternal and maternal grandparent couples.

## Calendar conflict

The handwriting is not the remaining problem: it reads *veinte y nueve*, not
an ambiguous numeral. The problem is that 1906 was not a leap year, so
29 February 1906 did not exist.

The public SSDI derivative's 28 February may be a later normalization to the
last valid day of the month. That is plausible, but it is not an independent
contemporary record and cannot silently correct the original.

The family account's 24 October 1906 and the 1941 marriage report of 24 October
1909 remain later conflicting reports. Childhood census ages continue to fit
1906 much better than 1909, but no reviewed source explains why 24 October was
remembered.

## Result

- February 1906: confirmed.
- Written original day: 29 February.
- Calendar validity: impossible.
- Possible correction to 28 February: plausible, unproved.
- Exact valid day: unresolved.

The next decisive source is a baptism, corrected civil copy, confirmation,
school, military, or travel record that independently gives Rafael's day of
birth.
