# Caguas burial register screen for Máximo Vázquez and his parents

## Question

Do the surviving early Caguas burials identify Máximo Vázquez, his father
Francisco, or his mother María Cortez, and do they state a birthplace that
would carry this line beyond Puerto Rico?

## Register and capture

The reviewed material is FamilySearch DGS **008126765**, item 9, from the
Catedral Dulce Nombre de Jesús in Caguas. The catalog groups the material as
death records for 1801–1810. The 190 photographed openings captured here
visibly run from entries in **January 1804** through entries in **June 1809**;
the manuscript sequence is not a substitute for the broader catalog label.

Every available opening in the item was saved:

- browser pages 41–230;
- film frames 01609–01798, using `frame = browser page + 1568`;
- 190 of 190 JPEG files present, with no missing page number;
- local archive:
  `research/pulls/scans/caguas-dgs-008126765-item9-burials-1801-1810/`; and
- SHA-256 of the sorted per-file SHA-256 list:
  `a3edfc3a4f06c681a6445565f978ce3ae9a4df0769d57122b0fd5107522b3a27`.

FamilySearch repeatedly downloads these images as `image.jpg`. Pages 60–112
were therefore recovered one at a time and renamed immediately, with the
embedded image identity checked against the expected film frame before the
next download.

## Review method

The full 190-image set received two Spanish handwriting-recognition passes.
A second pass split each photographed opening into two enlarged,
contrast-adjusted page columns. Target-name hints included:

- Máximo, Maximo, Maximino, and Maximiliano;
- Vázquez, Vazquez, Vásquez, Vasquez, Básquez, and Basquez;
- María Cortez and María Cortés; and
- Francisco.

Every plausible target-like result was checked against the original image.
The apparent hits resolved to other people or to badly recognized formulaic
language. Examples include:

- page 54: a supposed `Cortes` was text in an unrelated 1804 burial;
- page 59: Francisco Granquillo, a distinct named man about 73 years old;
- page 102: a child María in a different parent household;
- pages 139, 172, and 191: forms of sacramental language rather than a
  person named Máximo; and
- pages 170, 174–175, 178, 180, 196, and 198: unrelated children, adults,
  parents, or ordinary occurrences of María.

The OCR is too noisy to establish absence. This was a complete image capture
and a complete machine-assisted target screen with manual review of its
plausible hits; it was **not** a line-by-line human transcription of every
burial.

## Result

No defensible entry from this screen identifies:

- Máximo Vázquez or a secure name variant;
- the Francisco and María Cortez parent pair;
- a death for either parent; or
- an overseas birthplace for any of them.

This negative screen does not contradict the 1819 baptism that calls Máximo a
native of Caguas. It also does not prove that the people are absent from these
pages: handwriting recognition can miss a name, some entries are damaged or
faint, and death could have occurred outside the visible 1804–1809 span.

## Next step

Before treating the item as a definitive negative search, conduct a
line-by-line human transcription or locate a trustworthy contemporary index
or duplicate register. Earlier or later Caguas burial books, baptism gaps,
marriage dispensations, and diocesan copies remain higher-value routes to the
birthplaces of Francisco and María Cortez.
