# Francisco Xavier Vázquez of Cayey: candidate test

## Question

Could Francisco Vázquez, husband of María Magdalena Cortés in Caguas, be the Francisco Xavier Vázquez whose family appears in Cayey?

## Direct-line baseline

- Francisco Vázquez and María Magdalena are documented in Caguas by Miguel’s baptism in 1786.
- María Magdalena Cortés was buried there on 17 June 1794 as Francisco’s legitimate wife.
- Their son José was buried there on 14 February 1801, which shows Francisco was then living.
- No reviewed direct-line record names Francisco’s parents or native place.

## Candidate network

David M. Stark’s 2006 reconstruction identifies a Francisco Xavier Vázquez in Cayey as a son of Pedro Vázquez and Francisca Quiñones. It follows Pedro to Juan Vázquez de Rivera and Juliana de Alvarado. Juan was about twenty and resident in Ponce in 1709; the article explicitly says his ancestry is unknown.

Cayey transcriptions show Francisco Xavier and María de los Reyes with children in 1796 and 1799. That timing permits, but does not prove, a marriage after María Magdalena’s 1794 death.

## Original record test

The original Cayey burial page was downloaded at full size and visually reviewed. The 21 October 1801 entry names Francisco Xavier Vázquez and his wife María de los Reyes Vázquez. It does not name:

- his parents;
- a native place;
- an earlier wife;
- the Caguas children; or
- any other fact that identifies him as María Magdalena Cortés’s widower.

The archived image has SHA-256 `f743560fdc8ff305452ff9b3ce7c05310d7425dc67edd6bbbb0cd206cd2b5406`.

## Decision

Keep Francisco Xavier and the entire Cayey–Ponce network unmerged. The chronology makes the route worth testing, but it is not an ancestor line without an explicit Caguas-to-Cayey identity bridge.

Even if that bridge is eventually proved, the published reconstruction ends with Juan Vázquez de Rivera in Ponce and provides no pre-Puerto Rico birthplace or ancestry.

## Most discriminating next records

1. A marriage of Francisco Xavier and María de los Reyes that calls him the widower of María Magdalena Cortés.
2. A dispensation, military marriage inventory, burial, or duplicate record naming both his parents and a Caguas spouse or child.
3. A missing or diocesan Caguas record that names Francisco’s parents or native place.

The Archivo General de Indias unit `ESCRIBANIA,129A`, cited by Stark for Juan in 1709 and 1719, is cataloged by PARES as microfilmed but has no online image gallery. Inspecting it could check the published reading, but cannot by itself prove that the Cayey man belongs to the direct line.
