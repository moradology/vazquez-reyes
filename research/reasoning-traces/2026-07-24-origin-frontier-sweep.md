# Origin-frontier sweep: Caguas, Humacao, and Las Piedras

Date: 24 July 2026

## Scope

This was deliberately a shallow pass. The goal was to verify the earliest
people, relationships, places, and record classifications already in view, then
identify the honest edge of the evidence. Collateral families were not expanded
unless they helped test a parent pair or a place.

## What the originals establish

The 31 December 1805 Humacao marriage names **Máximo Basquez** and **Josefa
Ribera**. It calls Máximo the legitimate son of **Francisco** and **María
Cortez**, and Josefa the legitimate daughter of **Luís** and **Isidora
Rodríguez**. The entry gives no surname for Francisco or Luís.

Their son Juan de Dios's 1819 Juncos baptism supplies the couple's native
places:

- Máximo was a native of **Caguas, Puerto Rico**;
- Josefa was a native of **Humacao, Puerto Rico**.

The same entry describes them as *pardos libres*. That wording is retained as
the parish's colonial racial and legal classification: pardo and free. It does
not identify an African-born ancestor, a particular African place, a precise
ethnic mixture, or a modern ancestry percentage.

## A likely sister for Josefa

The complete Humacao marriage transcription identifies another 1805 bride with
the same parents. The original image was opened and downloaded:

- **Simona Ribera** married **León Garrafa** in Humacao on 24 April 1805;
- the entry calls Simona the legitimate daughter of **Luís** and **Isidora
  Rodríguez**.

The exact parent pair in the same parish and year, eight months before Josefa's
marriage, makes Simona a strong likely sister. Because the fathers' surnames are
not written in either original, the Rivera surname supplied for Luís in the
volunteer spreadsheet is treated as normalization and is not promoted.

## Early-book checks

### Caguas

The complete 1785–1788 baptism transcription contains no Máximo matching
parents Francisco and María Cortez. The complete 1774–1804 marriage
transcription contains no defensible Francisco and María Cortez couple.

These are limited negative results. The published Caguas guide shows large
baptism gaps in 1767–1784 and 1789–1808. A missing match in 1785–1788 therefore
does not contradict the original statement that Máximo was from Caguas.

### Humacao and Las Piedras

The surviving digitized Humacao baptism and marriage runs begin in 1793. The
first Humacao marriage book covers the period of Máximo and Josefa's marriage.
Its collection label groups pardos and enslaved residents, while the
family-specific 1819 baptism explicitly says that Máximo and Josefa were free.

The Las Piedras guide identifies early baptism coverage from 1778 and marriage
coverage from 1787. The complete 1787–1816 marriage transcription contains no
Luís and Isidora Rodríguez marriage. This is one negative search, not proof
that they married elsewhere: an earlier, missing, differently cataloged, or
neighboring register may hold the event.

The eastern-parish jurisdiction and coverage make Las Piedras the sensible next
place to test for Humacao-family baptisms and marriages before 1793.

## Current boundary

The verified direct-line boundary is:

- Máximo Vázquez — native of Caguas; parents Francisco and María Cortez;
- Josefa Rivera — native of Humacao; parents Luís and Isidora Rodríguez;
- no reviewed record gives a birthplace for those four parents;
- no reviewed record names a direct ancestor born in Africa, Europe, or another
  Caribbean island.

Those overseas routes remain open research possibilities. They are not current
family-history claims.
